Campaign Experiment: I Let the Car Decide Where I Go

Status: Concept Ready to Test
Category: Travel / Car Rental
Target Brand: DiscoverCars.com

The Problem

Renting a car isn't usually the exciting part of a trip. The road trip itself is.

But choosing the car can become a surprisingly annoying part of planning one. Different prices, different rental companies, different deposits, mileage rules, and rental conditions. Sometimes the cheapest-looking option isn't actually the one you'd choose after reading the details.

DiscoverCars.com already solves much of that by bringing rental offers together and letting travelers compare price, supplier ratings, rental conditions, deposits, mileage, fuel policies, and other details before booking. But here's the creative question:

How do we make people actually want to watch that comparison process happen?

The Idea: “I Let the Car Decide My Trip.”

Instead of starting with: “Today I'm going to show you how I booked a rental car…” we start with:

“I have no idea where I'm going today. The car I rent is going to decide.”

The creator chooses a destination. Then they open DiscoverCars.com. But they don't simply pick the cheapest car. They give themselves a set budget and a few rules:

  • A set budget (e.g., €50/day)
  • Must fit everything they need for the trip
  • Must make sense for the roads they'll be driving
  • Acceptable rental conditions and security deposits
  • Comes from a supplier they feel comfortable with

The car they choose becomes the creative starting point for the entire adventure.

The Creative Twist

Once the car is selected, the creator builds the trip directly around its personality. Got a tiny city hatchback? Maybe they stay on the coast and explore tiny historic villages. Got a rugged 4x4 SUV? They head straight into mountain passes. Got a convertible? The trip morphs into a scenic coastal drive. The vehicle isn't just transportation anymore; it's the creative director of the trip.

The Hero Video Structure

Title Idea: “I Let My Rental Car Decide My Entire Trip”

The video hooks the audience immediately by highlighting the spontaneous challenge rather than a product demonstration. We watch the creator query DiscoverCars.com, comparing prices, supplier ratings, and deposits in real time. The decisions they make on-screen teach the audience how to evaluate rental terms organically.

Why This Is More Interesting Than a Normal Sponsorship

The audience isn't being asked to care about car rentals. They're asking themselves: “What kind of trip is this person about to have based on their choice?”

Constraints build suspenseful storytelling. A smaller budget option vs. a practical utility option creates real tradeoffs that viewers can relate to.

The DiscoverCars Integration

Rather than a dry feature readout, the creator demonstrates comparisons naturally:

“This supplier is €10 cheaper per day, but look at the security deposit. This second option includes full coverage and unlimited mileage, making it way safer for our itinerary.”

By showing the decision process, the viewer learns the value of the platform without feeling like they are watching a commercial.

The Creator Persona

We target spontaneous travel vloggers, road-trippers, or budget-challenge creators. The key is storytelling ability—someone who can make comparing vehicle details feel like setting up a game-changing quest.

The Conversion Layer

At the end of the narrative, the creator directs viewers to a custom search result page replicating their route: “If you're planning a road trip, I used DiscoverCars to compare everything. Link is below to plan your own.”
