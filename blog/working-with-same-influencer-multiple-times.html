Can You Work With the Same Influencer More Than Once?

September 19, 2026 · 4 min read · Brands

A brand finds a creator.

The campaign goes well. The content performs. The audience responds.

So what happens next?

A lot of brands immediately start looking for another creator for the next campaign.

But there is another option that can be easier to overlook: work with the same influencer again.

And in many cases, that makes far more commercial sense than constantly starting from scratch.

01 Why Repeat Influencer Partnerships Work

The biggest advantage is familiarity.

The creator already knows your brand, your product, and what the collaboration workflow feels like. Your marketing team also knows what the creator's raw deliverables look like and how they communicate with their community.

You don't have to spend weeks onboarding each other or going back and forth on basic brand guidelines.

More importantly: the audience gets to see the brand more than once.

That matters tremendously. A single sponsored post can introduce someone to a product. Seeing that same creator naturally use, mention, or integrate the brand again months later builds authentic credibility and brand recall.

02 The Second Campaign Can Be Better Than the First

Your first collaboration with any creator is partly a learning exercise.

In that first flight, you uncover invaluable empirical data:

  • Which specific content format and hook engaged the audience
  • How the community reacted in comments
  • What messaging angles felt natural vs. forced
  • How many people clicked tracking links or used promo codes
  • Whether the creator delivered on time and was easy to manage
  • What could be tightened up in the script

The second campaign gives you the exact opportunity to capitalize on that data.

Maybe the first YouTube integration achieved 50,000 views but generated fewer link clicks than expected. Instead of writing the creator off, you can adjust the call-to-action, provide an exclusive landing page, or position the integration earlier in the video.

The first campaign gives you data. The next one gives you a chance to use it.

03 Don't Repeat the Exact Same Campaign

This is where brands often make a mistake.

If a creator makes three nearly identical sponsored segments reciting the same bullet points, their audience will quickly develop ad blindness and skip through.

The creator's content should always feel organic to their feed. You can keep the brand partnership consistent while evolving the narrative idea.

For example, a travel app working with a creator across four campaigns could structure the progression like this:

Campaign 1
How they plan an upcoming trip using the app
Campaign 2
Using the app in real time during an actual international trip
Campaign 3
A comparison between different travel booking and budget options
Campaign 4
A story about something that went wrong while traveling and how the app resolved it

Same creator. Same brand. Different reason to watch.

By changing the situational context, each video provides genuine entertainment while reinforcing your product.

04 When Should You Work With the Same Influencer Again?

Look beyond whether the post just scored high gross impressions. Ask what actually occurred:

  • Did the audience engage and discuss the product?
  • Did people click the trackable links?
  • Did the creator drive conversions, downloads, or signups?
  • Did the integration feel natural and aligned with their voice?
  • Was the creator professional, communicative, and timely?

You don't need every metric to be flawless. But there should be a strong baseline to build upon.

A creator who generated fewer views than estimated but drove highly qualified customers is far more valuable for a second campaign than a creator who scored massive empty reach with zero conversions.

05 Long-Term Partnerships vs. Repeated One-Offs

There is a meaningful difference between repeatedly booking one-off slots and negotiating an intentional long-term ambassador partnership.

One-Off Transactional Deal

“Make one video for us this month.” Creator has little skin in the game; feels like a standard sponsored interruption to viewers with zero compounding value.

Strategic Multi-Month Partnership

“Let’s partner over the next 6 months across 4 integrations.” Better bundle pricing, deeper creator buy-in, and genuine organic brand affinity.

For industries like travel, consumer tech, fitness, beauty, and food, repeated exposure is vital. Your product naturally fits different daily routines and seasonal moments.

06 What If the First Campaign Wasn't Amazing?

You don't necessarily have to write the creator off immediately.

Diagnose what actually underperformed:

  • Was the creative concept flawed? Maybe the format was too sales-heavy.
  • Was placement sub-optimal? Did the integration appear too late or disrupt the video flow?
  • Was the offer weak? Did the call-to-action lack a compelling discount or incentive?

The goal is to separate creator performance from campaign mechanics. One weak campaign doesn't always mean the creator was the wrong choice.

When Should You Move On?

Repeat partnerships aren't a universal mandate. If the audience showed no interest, performance was consistently low, communication was painful, or their audience demographic shifted, it makes sense to reallocate budget.

The objective isn't to work with the same creator forever—it is to recognize and cultivate the high-performing partnerships that compound in value.

The Simple Takeaway

Influencer marketing doesn't have to be a hamster wheel of:

Find creator → Sponsor post → Move on → Start from scratch

The brands scaling profitably in 2026 operate with this model:

Find creator → Test → Learn → Optimize → Work together again

When you find the right creator fit, a repeat relationship is almost always more profitable and seamless than starting over from zero.
