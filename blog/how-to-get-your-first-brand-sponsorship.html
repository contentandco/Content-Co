How to Get Your First Brand Sponsorship as a Creator

August 28, 2026 · 3 min read

Getting your first brand deal can feel like a chicken-and-egg problem.

You need brands to notice you. But it feels like brands won't notice you until you've already worked with brands.

The good news is that you don't need 100,000 followers or a long list of sponsorships to start. You need to make it easy for a brand to look at your content and think:

“I can see this person working for us.”

Here's where to start.

01 Make your content easy to understand

Before a brand contacts you, they should be able to figure out what you actually create.

If your profile is travel, make that obvious. If you create tech reviews, make that obvious. If you create productivity content, the same applies.

Brands aren't just looking at your follower count. They're trying to understand whether your content and audience make sense for their campaign.

You don't need to fit into one tiny category forever. But having a clear starting point makes you much easier to work with.

02 You don't need a huge audience

This is probably the biggest misconception about brand deals.

A smaller creator can be valuable if they have the right audience and consistently create good content.

Imagine a travel creator with 25,000 followers whose audience is heavily interested in budget travel. For a travel app aimed at people planning affordable trips, that could be much more useful than a general lifestyle creator with 200,000 followers.

The question isn't just “How many people follow you?”

It's “Who are those people, and why do they listen to you?”

03 Have a simple media kit ready

You don't want to spend three days making a 20-page presentation. A useful media kit can be simple.

Include:

  • Who you are and what you create
  • Your main platforms and collaboration ideas
  • Audience demographics and average views or reach
  • Engagement or other useful performance numbers
  • A few examples of your best content

The goal is to answer the questions a brand is already asking: Who is this creator? Who watches them? What can they create for us?

04 Start pitching brands yourself

You don't have to wait for brands to find you.

Find companies you already use or genuinely think would make sense for your audience. Then send a short email.

Don't write:

“Hello, I have 50K followers and would love to collaborate.”

Give them a reason to care. Try something closer to:

“I've been using your app on my recent trips, and I had an idea for a short video showing how I use it when planning a trip.”

You're not just asking for money. You're bringing an idea.

05 Show brands what you could make

One of the easiest ways to make yourself more attractive to a brand is to remove some of the guesswork.

If you're pitching a travel company, don't just say you want to collaborate. Give them an idea. Maybe:

“3 things I wish I knew before my first trip to Japan.”

Or:

“How I planned a week in Europe without spending a fortune.”

Then explain where their product could naturally fit. You don't need to give away a full campaign strategy for free. Just show that you understand how your content and their product could work together.

06 Treat your profile like a portfolio

A brand might see your pitch and immediately check your profile. What will they find?

If your last ten posts are random topics, inconsistent quality, and nothing related to the audience you're pitching, the pitch becomes much harder.

You don't need every post to be perfect. But your recent content should give a brand confidence that you can actually deliver. Your content is your portfolio.

08 And don't forget disclosure

If a brand pays you, gives you a free product, or provides another benefit in exchange for content, you may have disclosure obligations.

For example, the FTC says a material connection with a brand should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, and creators shouldn't rely on vague wording that audiences may not understand.

The exact rules depend on where you and your audience are located, so it's worth checking the rules that apply to you.

09 Your first deal doesn't have to be your biggest deal

Your first sponsorship is mostly about proving that you can do this professionally.

Create something good. Communicate well. Meet the deadline. Make the partnership feel natural.

Then you have something much more valuable than a number in your follower count:

A real example of what you can do for a brand.

That's how you start turning one sponsorship into the next one.

A real example of what you can do for a brand is much more valuable than a number in your follower count. Build trust first, scale later.
