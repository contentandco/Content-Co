How to Find the Right Influencers for Your Brand

August 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Finding influencers isn't difficult anymore.

You can search Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, or use an influencer platform and find thousands of creators in minutes.

The difficult part is figuring out which ones are actually worth your time and budget.

A creator can have 200,000 followers and still be a poor fit for your brand. Another with 20,000 followers might have exactly the audience you want.

So how do you tell the difference?

Start with your customer, not the influencer

Before searching for creators, think about who you actually want to reach.

Where do they live? What are they interested in? What kind of content do they watch?

If you're a travel brand, for example, you probably want someone whose audience is already interested in travel—not simply someone with a large following.

The closer the creator's audience is to your customer, the more useful the partnership can be.

Don't let follower count make the decision

Follower count is easy to see, so it's tempting to use it as your first filter.

Don't.

Look at recent views, engagement, and the quality of the conversations happening around their content.

A creator with 30,000 followers who regularly gets strong views and thoughtful comments can be much more valuable than a creator with 300,000 followers whose audience barely interacts.

The goal isn't to reach the most people.

It's to reach the right people.

Watch their recent content

Don't make your decision from the creator's profile page alone.

Watch their recent videos. Read their captions. Look at the comments.

Ask yourself:

Would this creator naturally talk about my product?

If every piece of content feels completely unrelated to your brand, the partnership may feel forced.

But if your product fits naturally into what they already create, the promotion has a much better chance of feeling like something their audience would actually want to hear about.

Look at their previous brand deals

Past sponsorships can tell you a lot.

Have they worked with brands similar to yours? Did the sponsored content feel natural? Do they promote a different product every few days?

You're not just hiring someone's audience.

You're also choosing someone to represent your brand.

Take a few minutes to understand how they handle partnerships before sending an offer.

Make a shortlist, not a giant spreadsheet

You don't need hundreds of names.

Start with a smaller group of creators who genuinely look like a fit.

For each one, check:

  • Does their audience match our customer?
  • Does their content fit our brand?
  • Are their recent views consistent?
  • Does their audience actually engage?
  • Do their previous partnerships make sense?
  • Can I imagine our product appearing naturally in their content?

If the answer is mostly yes, you've probably found someone worth contacting.

The right influencer isn't always the biggest one

This is probably the most important thing to remember.

You're not looking for the biggest creator. You're looking for the right creator.

A smaller creator with a focused audience, strong content, and genuine credibility can be a better partner than a much larger creator who has little connection to your target customer.

Once you start looking at influencers this way, finding creators becomes less about collecting follower counts and more about finding people who can actually make sense for your brand.

A simple rule to remember

Right audience → right content → real engagement → natural fit.

Get those four things right, and you've already done much of the hard work.
