Influencer Outreach: What to Say When You First Contact a Creator

September 5, 2026 · 4 min read · For Brands

Finding the right creator is only half the job.

You can have a great shortlist and still get almost no replies if your first message feels like it was copied and sent to 200 people.

Creators get plenty of brand emails every single week. Most of them are easy to ignore.

So what should you actually say?

01 Start with why you picked them

Don't open with a long introduction about your company.

Tell them why you're reaching out to them specifically.

❌ Instead of (Generic & Self-Centered)

“We're a leading travel company looking to collaborate with creators across the world.”

✓ Try this (Specific & Human)

“I've been watching your travel videos and really liked your recent content around budget trips.”

It's a small difference, but it tells the creator that you actually looked at their content and didn't just scrape their email from a generic database.

02 Keep the first message short

Your first email doesn't need to explain the entire campaign, your brand's founding backstory, or a 10-page creative brief.

You mainly need to answer three simple questions:

  • Why them? (What about their channel fits)
  • Why you? (What brand or product you represent)
  • What are you asking for? (A quick conversation to see if it makes sense)
Sample First Contact Pitch

“We're working on an upcoming campaign for a travel app and think your audience could be a good fit. Would you be open to hearing more about it?”

That's enough to start a conversation. You can discuss deliverables, rates, timelines, and content ideas once they confirm they're interested.

03 Don't pretend every creator is your perfect match

Creators can tell immediately when an email is generic.

If you're contacting a travel creator, mention something real and verifiable about their content:

  • Maybe they make city guides for solo backpackers.
  • Maybe they focus on long-form van-life vlogs.
  • Maybe their audience is passionate about flight hacks and budget routes.

Use that context. One genuine sentence is worth more than five paragraphs of polished corporate language.

04 Give them enough information to decide

Once a creator responds showing interest, don't make them play email ping-pong for a week just to understand the scope.

Lay out the basics clearly:

  • Brand: Who you are and what product you're promoting.
  • Campaign: The central theme or objective.
  • Content Format: Dedicated video, 60s integration, Shorts/Reels, or UGC.
  • Approximate Timeline: Publishing window and review deadlines.
  • Main Deliverables: Exact deliverables expected.
  • Budget or Rate Expectations: What budget bracket is allocated, when appropriate.
  • Any Important Requirements: Exclusivity, whitelisting rights, or geographic restrictions.

The creator should be able to quickly decide whether the opportunity aligns with their calendar and audience.

05 Don't make the first message all about your brand

This is easy to forget when writing outreach campaigns.

The creator doesn't really care that your company was founded in 2018, won an industry award last year, or has users in 50 countries.

They care about whether the partnership makes sense for them and provides real value to their audience.

Lead with the opportunity, not your company history.

06 And don't give up after one email

Sometimes a creator doesn't reply simply because they're busy filming, traveling, or buried under inbox overload.

That doesn't automatically mean they're not interested.

A simple, polite follow-up 3 to 4 days later can double your response rate.

The Frictionless Follow-Up

“Just wanted to follow up on this in case it got buried in your inbox. No pressure at all, just let me know if you might be interested!”

No guilt. No pressure. Just a clean reminder.

The goal isn't to send more emails

It's to start better conversations.

You could send 100 generic pitches and get nowhere. Or send 30 well-researched ones and find several creators who are genuinely interested and enthusiastic to work together.

The difference usually starts before you even press send.

“Find the right creator. Give them a reason to care. Keep the message human.”
