Why Your Travel Influencer Campaign Isn't Working

August 26, 2026 · 3 min read

You found the creators.

You paid for the content.

The posts went live.

And then… not much happened.

If that sounds familiar, it doesn't necessarily mean influencer marketing doesn't work for your travel brand.

Sometimes the problem is the campaign itself.

01 You chose creators because they looked impressive

A big following can make a creator look like an obvious choice.

But travel is personal. Someone might have hundreds of thousands of followers who simply aren't interested in what you're selling.

A smaller creator with an audience that actually plans trips, searches for hotels, compares flights, or looks for things to do can be much more useful.

Don't ask:

“How many people will see this?”

Also ask:

“How many of the right people will care?”

02 The content feels like an advertisement

Travel content usually works best when people can imagine themselves taking the trip.

If the creator spends the entire video reading talking points about your hotel or app, it can quickly feel like an ad.

Give creators the important things you need them to communicate, but let them tell the story in their own way.

Their audience follows them for a reason.

03 You measured the wrong thing

Views are useful. So are likes and comments.

But they don't always tell you whether the campaign did anything for the business.

  • For a booking platform, you might care about clicks and bookings.
  • For a hotel, it could be enquiries, bookings, or increased searches.
  • For a destination, it might be interest in a specific location.

The right metric depends on what you wanted the campaign to achieve in the first place.

04 You treated the campaign like a one-off post

Someone watches a travel video today and books a trip three months later.

That's very different from selling a product someone can buy immediately.

Travel decisions can take time.

That's one reason longer creator relationships, repeat content, affiliate links, or organizing comprehensive influencer brand trips can be more useful than simply paying for one post and moving on.

05 You gave the creator too much—or too little—direction

A creator shouldn't have to guess what the campaign is about. But they also shouldn't have to follow a script word for word.

Give them the important information: What are we trying to achieve? Who are we trying to reach? What needs to be mentioned?

Then let them figure out how to make it work for their audience.
